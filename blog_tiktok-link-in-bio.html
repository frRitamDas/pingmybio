TikTok link in bio — how to add it (and what to put there)

The tappable Website field, the personal-account trap, and why that one URL should answer and book — not dump people into a list. 2026·09·09 · 9 min read

Google’s first result for TikTok link in bio is a Reddit thread: “Can’t add a link in my TikTok bio.” The rest is TikTok’s help doc, Shopify/Bitly/Later how-tos, and YouTube walkthroughs. This guide covers the TikTok steps first (check TikTok’s official help — rules change by region), then the question nobody answers: what should actually live at that URL?

Short answer: Add the URL in Edit profile → Website on the phone app. If the field is missing, you’re usually on a personal account under TikTok’s follower/business rules — or in a region where the field hasn’t been rolled out. Paste a full URL including https://. Description text is not the Website field; it won’t be tappable.

What “link in bio” means on TikTok

On TikTok, “link in bio” means the website field under your bio text on your profile — the one place a normal outbound URL can live, since captions and comments don’t carry clickable links the way YouTube descriptions do. It’s not a special product. It’s one URL slot plus whatever page you point it at. Treat it like prime real estate: one page, one job.

How to add a TikTok link in bio

  1. Do this on the TikTok mobile app. Desktop usually can’t set the clickable Website field.
  2. Open your profile (the person icon).
  3. Tap Edit profile.
  4. Find Website (not just Bio) and paste a full URL with https://.
  5. Save, open your profile as a viewer would, and tap it to confirm.

Official path for connecting accounts: TikTok Support — link a website.

Can’t add a link? The usual causes

  • Personal account, too small. TikTok has historically gated the Website field by account type and follower count. Either grow past the threshold or switch to a Business account (Edit profile → Switch to Business Account) where the field is generally available.
  • Region limits. The field rolls out unevenly. If support says your region lacks it, your only honest option is the bio text + “link in bio” pin comment pointing to your page.
  • URL formatting. Missing protocol, spaces, or a shortened link that trips the filter. Use the clean canonical URL.
  • App version. Update TikTok. Stale builds lose fields.

What to put in that one slot

The failure mode is pointing the field at a dead Linktree graveyard and hoping. TikTok sends you impulse traffic — someone who just watched 34 seconds of you. They will not navigate a sitemap.

  • If you sell one thing — the checkout page. Nothing else.
  • If you sell services — a page that answers “how much / what’s included / can we talk?” before a human has to. That’s an assistant page, not a list.
  • If you’re building an audience — the lead magnet or newsletter, with the assistant qualifying who should get what.

This is the design brief for PingMyBio: one URL that greets, answers, recommends the right offer, and takes the booking or collab request — while you’re still editing the next video.

Make the slot compound

  • Say it out loud in videos: “link in bio” lands once you give people a reason.
  • Keep the URL stable. Changing it means every old video points at a ghost.
  • Watch your click analytics weekly — the pages that work tell you what your audience actually wants next.
30-second audit: open your profile in a private browser. Can a stranger, in under ten seconds, (1) understand what you do, (2) get an answer to the most common question, and (3) take the next step — without DM-ing you? If not, fix that before posting another video.
