How to set up an AI storefront for your link in bio

Profile, knowledge, offers, FAQs, Work with me — the exact setup, and how to go live on Instagram or TikTok. 2026·09·09 · 8 min read

Most “set up your link in bio” guides end with a stack of buttons. That is a classic link page: list URLs, hope people pick the right one, answer the rest in DMs. An AI storefront is different. Followers tap one link, talk to an assistant grounded in your knowledge, open your offers, or start a Work with me request — without bouncing through three other apps. That is what PingMyBio is built for.

Short answer: Sign up → claim pingmybio.com/you → fill Profile → add Knowledge and FAQs → publish offers (and Work with me if brands matter) → paste the URL into Instagram/TikTok. Skip the theme rabbit hole; spend the time on answers the assistant can use.

PingMyBio runs as an AI bio at runtime — not a Classic/AI toggle in settings. If you only need a pretty button list, use a list tool. If your bio is how you get clients, keep reading.

What you need before you start

  • An email you check (login + Inbox alerts)
  • A short bio and a clear photo or brand mark
  • At least one offer you actually sell — a linked offer, a freebie, or Work with me
  • The answers you already repeat in DMs (“how much?”, “what’s included?”, “do you work with brands?”)

Step 1 — Create your page and claim the URL

  1. Go to Create a page and sign up.
  2. Claim a short slug. Your public page will be pingmybio.com/yourname.
  3. Use the handle people already know from Instagram or TikTok when you can. Changing it later means updating every bio that links to you.

Step 2 — Finish Profile (the storefront header)

Open App → Profile. This is what fans see before they chat: your name, one-line bio, avatar, and category. Keep the bio to one sharp sentence — “Coaching for creators who sell” beats a paragraph. Finish identity here first; everything else reads better once the header is real.

Step 3 — Feed Knowledge (this is the moat)

Knowledge is the raw material your assistant answers from. Paste in the things you’d tell a new client on a first call: what you sell, how it works, pricing anchors, turnaround times, policies, the story behind it. No need to write “documentation” — bullet notes beat essays. Then add your FAQs as individual entries; the assistant retrieves them verbatim when a fan asks.

Step 4 — Publish offers

Create offers for the paths you want fans to take: your main program, a low-ticket freebie, a workshop, 1:1 time. Order matters — the assistant surfaces higher-priority offers first when the moment fits. Each offer carries a title, a price or “free”, a short pitch, and a destination (checkout, calendar, or external link).

Step 5 — Turn on Work with me (if brands matter)

Work with me puts a brand-facing intake on your page: scope, budget, timeline. When a partner submits, PingMyBio runs research and drops a reply brief into your Inbox — company snapshot, fit analysis, and a suggested first reply. You keep the final say; the machine does the homework.

Step 6 — Go live

  1. Copy your public URL from the dashboard.
  2. Instagram: Edit profile → Links → Add external link.
  3. TikTok: Edit profile → Website (phone app only — full walkthrough).
  4. Pin it, and tell your audience what not to do: don’t ask in comments, ask on the page.
The 20-minute version: Profile (4 min) → Knowledge: paste your last 10 DM replies (8 min) → one flagship offer (4 min) → bio link swap (2 min). Ship it. Refine weekly.

Common mistakes

  • Over-theming. Nobody fans out over gradients. Answers close; aesthetics convince.
  • Empty Knowledge. An assistant with nothing to say says “I don’t know” — and fans leave.
  • Ten offers at once. Start with three paths: buy, book, or freebie. Expand later.
  • Linking the wrong URL. Your bio should anchor to the page, not a campaign that expires.
